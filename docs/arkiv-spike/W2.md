## Retention cost per email per year

Max lifetime is 182.5 days, so a year needs: create(max) + extend at ~day 180 + extend at ~day 360.
- 558,180 + 2 x 420,613 = 1,399,406 gas = 0.0014 tGLM per email per year (extending later than needed wastes nothing; each extend re-bases to now+max).
- Cheaper alternative: create with 1 h/short lifetime (180k) is only for OTPs, not mail.
- Per 1,000,000 emails: ~1,400 tGLM per year at today's flat 1 gwei (mainnet pricing will differ). Batching extends in `executeBatch` would amortize the 21k base tx cost but per-entity cost dominates (~420k); not measured.

## Conclusions for Nanite

OTP expiry demo: feasible and quite precise. Expiry is block-based (2 s blocks), exact to the block: `$expiresAt` = landing block + N. The entity vanished from both `query` and `getEntity` within ~2 blocks (4 s) of its deadline in our test (limited by 10 s polling). Wall clock: 60 s asked, 30 blocks, and the observed wall time was 60 s +/- poll jitter. Minimum useful OTP TTL is a few blocks (durations must be multiples of 2 s); 60 s demo works. Caveats: public RPC lags a block or two; do not rely on exact-second semantics in UI, show "expires in about 1 min". Expiry is silent (no event), so a UI must poll or compute from `$expiresAt`. Expiry does NOT remove calldata from chain history: the creating tx input (ciphertext) stays readable. That is fine for encrypted mail, but means "deleted" on expiry or `deleteEntity` is only state-level, not erased-from-chain. For OTPs, keep the OTP value encrypted anyway.

Retention design:
- Chain max lifetime is 182.5 days, so long-lived mail REQUIRES a recurring extend job (at least every ~150-170 days with margin; an expired entity cannot be revived).
- Who extends: the owner can always extend. If the entity is user-owned (after changeOwnership), the operator cannot extend unless the entity was created with `permissionlessExtension`. With the flag, any account (the Nanite operator, a stranger) can extend, confirmed with index 7. The flag is immutable, so decide at creation. Recommended: create every mail entity with `permissionlessExtension` and transfer to the user, so an operator cron can keep it alive without holding write access to content (a stranger still cannot patch or delete, tested).
- Risk of anyone being able to extend: extension only moves the expiry later (shortening reverts), cannot edit or delete. Worst case a third party spends their own gas to keep someone's (encrypted) mail alive past the user's intent, and the user cannot shorten it themselves, since shortening reverts for everyone, owner included. If "expiring on schedule" matters (OTPs, right-to-erasure), do not set the flag on those entities. Also a stranger could extend to max, so lifetime is only user-controlled by `deleteEntity`. Cost to attacker is ~0.0004 tGLM per entity.
- Cron: the job can find extendable entities with `$expiresAt < now + threshold` filtered by type (query by `$expiresAt` is supported) and extend to `max()`; it needs funded gas on one wallet. Missed windows lose data permanently, so alert on job failure.
- Gas gotcha: one extend returned "ran out of gas ... nothing applied" (SDK estimate stale); retry fixed it. The cron must retry.
